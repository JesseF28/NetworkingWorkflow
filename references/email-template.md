# Jesse's outreach email

Taken from emails Jesse wrote and sent (tracker sheet, "Outreach drafts 2019-2026" tab). Match it closely: same opening, same length, same sign-off. Personalize only the two marked sentences. `school_year`, `major` and the availability line come from `config.json`, so if Jesse becomes a sophomore, only the config changes.

## Template

**Subject:** Vanderbilt freshman interested in {private equity | venture capital | growth equity | hedge funds}

```
Hi {First name},

I hope you're doing well! My name is Jesse Fagen, and I'm a freshman at Vanderbilt studying mathematics and economics. I came across your profile and was interested in {HOOK: one specific, true detail about their path or role}.

I'd love to hear {QUESTION: what Jesse wants to learn from someone in their seat}.

{availability line from config.json, verbatim}

Thank you for your time, and I hope we can connect!

Best,
Jesse Fagen
```

Use the name the person goes by (firm bio / LinkedIn), e.g. "Rob" not "Robert" if the bio says Rob.

## What makes a good hook and question

- The hook names something concrete: a prior firm, the firm's niche (GP stakes, sports & media PE, lower-middle-market industrials, healthcare VC), their Vanderbilt major, a recent promotion or deal. Grammatically it continues "I was interested in…" or "I was interested to see that…".
- The question is something this person is uniquely placed to answer, and it's about learning, not asking for a job. Vary it across the 20 emails; the same question pasted 20 times reads as a mail-merge.
- Keep each personalized sentence to one sentence. The whole email should stay ~120–150 words like the examples.
- Don't claim things Jesse didn't do ("I read your thesis on…") and don't invent facts. If the research only supports "you studied economics at Vanderbilt and are now an associate at X", use that.
